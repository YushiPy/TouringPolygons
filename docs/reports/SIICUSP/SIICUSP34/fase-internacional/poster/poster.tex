  \block{background-head}{42.7}{18.3}{38.4}{\head{2}{Background and contribution}}
  \block{background}{42.7}{20.67}{38.4}{\tagtext A polynomial subproblem\par\vspace{0.15cm}\bodytext Both variants are NP-hard for non-convex polygons, but the fixed-order TPP with convex polygons is polynomial: Dror et al. [1] use last-step shortest-path maps, improved by Tan and Jiang [2]. These algorithms fix $s$ and $t$; a TSPN tour has no fixed point.\par\vspace{0.35cm}\tagtext The state of the art\par\vspace{0.15cm}\bodytext Fekete et al. [3] solve the TSPN by branch and bound. Each convex subproblem is a second-order cone program (SOCP) solved by Gurobi, a commercial optimizer, within a tolerance.\par\vspace{0.35cm}\tagtext Our contribution\par\vspace{0.15cm}\bodytext An exact algorithm for the \textbf{fixed-order closed tour} through convex polygons, which may touch or overlap. An optimal tour touches some boundary, so we scan boundary segments, solve anchored paths with the maps of [1] and use the convexity of the anchored length. On rational input the tour is optimal with \textbf{zero error}. For speed, a floating-point stage proves bounds with directed rounding; the exact stage runs only when they are not tight enough.}

  \block{method-head}{34.0}{41.4}{47.1}{\head{4}{Branch and bound}}
  \block{method}{34.0}{43.6}{47.1}{\bodytext A node holds a partial sequence of $m$ targets, each one its \textbf{convex hull} or one \textbf{convex piece} of an optimal decomposition by Greene's algorithm [4]. The node's closed tour is a \textbf{lower bound} $L$ for every extension, since omitting targets cannot lengthen a route. If the tour visits every polygon, it is feasible and may improve the best length $U$. Nodes with $L \ge U$ are pruned. The insertion and refinement rules follow [3].}
